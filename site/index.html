The money model

Attribution, not custody. The merchant is always paid in full.

Split402 sits beside standard x402 settlement and records who earned what. It never intercepts the payment and never holds the merchant's money.

01 · PAY

The agent pays normally

A buyer or agent settles the x402 USDC request. The merchant receives the gross payment exactly as before — nothing about settlement changes.

02 · ATTRIBUTE

A signed claim is attached

The referrer's signed referral claim rides along with the request. Split402 writes a merchant-signed receipt and verifies the settlement on-chain.

03 · PAY OUT

The referrer is paid later

The commission becomes an auditable payable. A payout worker moves accrued USDC from a merchant-controlled funding wallet — on the merchant's schedule.

Who it's for

Two sides, one signed receipt between them.

If you sell an API to agents, you gain distribution. If you route agents to paid APIs, you earn on every payment you drive. Neither side has to trust the other — the receipt is the proof.

Why it holds up

Every referral is a cryptographic artifact.

01Signed offers & receipts

Merchants sign the offer and the settlement receipt. Referrers sign the claim. Attribution is verifiable end to end.

02On-chain verification

The control plane confirms the underlying Solana USDC settlement before a commission ever becomes payable.

03Zero-sum ledger

Merchant liability, referrer payable, and protocol fee balance to zero on every accepted receipt. Nothing is invented.

04Stop-loss controls

Pause campaigns, payout wallets, and routes — all reversible, all over the API, no database access required.

What Split402 is not — read this first.

  • Not an on-chain payment splitter. The MVP records a commission liability funded by a later, merchant-controlled payout — it does not atomically split the payment on-chain.
  • Referrers carry merchant risk. A receipt proves a signed obligation, not that the merchant's payout wallet is funded. Solvency and payout-timing risk are real and disclosed.
  • Devnet, public alpha. No mainnet approval, no production custody, no commercial terms yet. Build and evaluate — don't run real-money campaigns.
